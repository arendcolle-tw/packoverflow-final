# Pitch: the story

What we pitch, what we say, what we show and what we leave out. Version 2 by Wout, 20 Oct around 14:00. When #hackathon-finale decides otherwise, the channel wins and this file gets updated.

**What changed in version 2.** Feedback on the first deck: too focused on the connector (MCP), and what does a business leader gain in money and time? Both point at the same fix. The connector is *how* people reach it, not *why* anyone pays for it. So the story now leads with what a leader gains, then how we do it, and the connector is one line.

## In one breath

AI now does a growing share of the work at SD Worx. PackOverflow shows a business leader where their people still understand that work and where nobody does anymore. Then they can let AI take more where it's safe, and catch the expensive mistake where it isn't. Nobody does extra work for it, because it learns from the AI work people already do.

- **Hook:** "The author is no longer the expert."
- **Tagline:** "Your AI can answer. We show who still understands."
- **Business line:** "We put human understanding on the balance sheet: where it is, and how fast it depreciates."
- **5-second visual:** two names side by side. Left, in blue: who you'd ask (the most active person, the doc owner). Right, in amber: who actually knows (corrected the AI, with their reason in their own words). Same color logic as `DESIGN.md`: blue is the machine, amber is the human who knows.

## Who pays, and what they get

The buyer is the leader who runs work that AI now does a big part of. At SD Worx, that's whoever runs agentic payroll: 500 Belgian consultants coordinating AI agents for 30,000+ companies. They get three things.

### 1. Time: the right person in seconds, not after a day of asking around

- People lose 5.3 hours a week waiting for knowledge from colleagues or recreating what someone already knew (Panopto with YouGov, 2018, US knowledge workers).
- **Back of the envelope:** if PackOverflow saves each of those 500 consultants one hour a week, that's 500 × 1 × 46 weeks = **23,000 hours a year, about 14 full-time consultants**. At an assumed €50 an hour loaded cost, that's about €1.15M a year.
- These are assumptions, not measurements: one hour out of the 5.3, 46 working weeks, 1,600 productive hours per full-time person, €50 an hour. Say "if", and say a pilot would measure it.

### 2. Risk: see where AI runs with nobody who understands it

- **Errors.** Fixing one payroll error costs about $339 on average (EY analysis for Paycom, 2026, US, errors only, compliance costs excluded). When an agent applies a wrong rule across a client portfolio, that cost repeats on every payslip. "Going dark" points at exactly the places where no human would catch it.
- **People leaving.** Replacing an employee costs half to two times their annual salary, mostly in ramp-up (Gallup). When a consultant leaves, the successor sees who really reasoned about each client. Single points of knowledge show up before the resignation, not after.
- **AI Act.** Organisations that deploy a high-risk AI system must give its oversight to people with "the necessary competence, training and authority" (Article 26(2)). Breaking that falls in the fine tier of up to €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover (Article 99(4)). We show where that competence still exists. Whether a given payroll agent counts as high-risk is a legal call, so don't claim it on stage.

### 3. Steering: where to automate more, and where to keep people sharp

- **Healthy** (several people still reason about it): safe to let the agent take more.
- **Going dark** (nobody lately, or only one person): train, rotate, or keep a human on it.
- 30% of HR leaders say a missing business case blocks AI (SD Worx HR & Payroll Pulse 2026). This is that business case, made visible. It turns "humans validate where it matters" from a promise into a number.

### What it costs to run

No knowledge base to maintain and no documentation to write. It reads the AI sessions people already have. That is the finals case's "zero unnecessary effort", in money.

### The next step we propose

A four-week pilot with one agentic-payroll team. We measure two things: time from question to the right person, and how many topics are going dark. Measurable customer outcomes are what SD Worx's strategy asks of innovation.

## How we do it (why we can and others can't)

Every way a company finds "who knows" runs on footprint: who wrote the doc, who owns it, who answered the thread. Expert finders and skill inference all work like this.

AI breaks footprint. When AI writes the doc, the email and the Slack answer, the author can be the person who understands it least. Output stopped being evidence of knowledge.

What AI can't fake is the moment a human overrules it, decides, or explains why. That's where knowledge shows, and it's the only thing we count. It fades over about 90 days unless someone reasons about it again.

Against the field: most teams score documents on freshness, owner and conflicts. We say documents will be fine, because AI writes flawless ones. What quietly disappears is understanding. That's the brief's "think beyond documents", taken literally.

## How people use it (a side note, not a slide)

- **Recording** runs in the background on the AI sessions people already have. Raw sessions stay on the laptop, and only the reasoning moments leave it.
- **A consultant** asks in the tool they already use: Slack (`/whoknows`) or their AI assistant. They get a person, that person's reason in their own words, and how recent it is.
- **A leader** sees one view: what's going dark, by team and topic.
- **Each person** privately sees what they actually know and what they only prompted.
- Under the hood it's a standard connector (MCP), so any agent can ask it, including SD Worx's own. **That's a Q&A answer, not a slide.**

## Where it goes: 10 to 20 years

- AI does most of the work and facts are free. The scarce asset becomes the person who can step in when the agent is wrong, a law flips overnight (Pay Transparency did), or a system breaks. Like any asset, a leader needs to know where it is and how fast it depreciates.
- Understanding erodes silently in three ways:
  - **Dormant systems** nobody has reasoned about in years, until an incident.
  - **The AI slop loop:** AI-written text feeds the next AI answer, and the human signal thins out.
  - **The broken apprenticeship:** who is SD Worx's senior PC 200 expert in 2040 if no junior reasons through a case in 2026?
- **Pilots solved this.** Under EASA rules (FCL.060) a pilot needs three take-offs and landings in the last 90 days before carrying passengers, because autopilot erodes skill. People who oversee AI have no such logbook. PackOverflow is that logbook, and our fade already runs on 90 days.
- **Every correction does two jobs:** it teaches the agent (SD Worx's own Legal Watch principle, experts' corrections feed back into the system) and it proves who knows.
- **Agents become users.** When a payroll agent isn't sure, it asks "who knows" and escalates to the right human.
- **For SD Worx's customers,** the same view becomes a critical-knowledge and succession product for 100,000+ organisations, on top of mysdworx.

## The 3 minutes

Beats, not a script. We get cut off on time, so rehearse to 2:45.

1. **0:00 to 0:20, the moment of doubt.** A payroll agent did the job. Something about it is off. Who here still understands it? The author is the obvious pick, but the AI wrote it.
2. **0:20 to 0:45, the insight.** "The author is no longer the expert." Only overruling, deciding and explaining why count.
3. **0:45 to 1:30, the demo.** Ask "who knows" in Slack and get the two names, with the real why ("retry 3 times, not 5, because the bank closes the SFTP window at 18:00"). Then the leader view: what's going dark on our own team (the Aikido scan).
4. **1:30 to 2:15, what a leader gets.** Time, risk, steering. One number: 23,000 hours a year, if it saves each agentic-payroll consultant one hour a week.
5. **2:15 to 2:40, 10 to 20 years.** Human understanding on the balance sheet. The pilot logbook. Agents asking for the right human.
6. **2:40 to 3:00, the close.** "Humans validate where it matters" only holds while they still understand. The four-week pilot. Tagline.

## Words

**Say:** who knows, going dark, in their own words, just accepted, corrected, the why, hours, risk, where to automate.

**Don't say on stage:** MCP, connector, install, knowledge graph, proficiency, decay, takeover, signals, rows, task versus topic, TF-IDF, dark, fading and single point as three separate states (say "going dark").

## What we show and what we don't

**Show:** the moment of doubt, the two names, a live "who knows" in Slack with a real why, the leader's "going dark" view, then the business case and the vision. The two-names beat needs one real topic where the busiest person is not the one who knows.

**Don't show:** how to install or add the connector ("try it yourself" belongs in a QR code on the last slide at most), the inside of the pipeline (record, graph and connector boxes), scores, the fade maths, or extra sources (screen capture, screenshots of other AI chats, Copilot, M365, meetings). Each one is a roadmap line or a Q&A answer.

## Do's and don'ts

**Do**
- Lead with what a leader gains (time, risk, steering), then how. Delivery is one sentence.
- Say what it is and show it working in the first 45 seconds. The organisers' feedback on round 1: it wasn't clear what we actually had.
- Label every number: a source, or "if".
- Demo on real sessions and say so.
- Put the live URL, screenshots and a recording of the demo path in the PDF. We pitch on the organisers' device.
- Use SD Worx words: payroll consultant, client portfolio, PC 200, agentic payroll, Legal Watch, mysdworx.
- Mark every illustration as one.

**Don't**
- Make the connector the hero. Replace "MCP" with "Slack bot" and the pitch should still work. If it doesn't, the pitch is about plumbing.
- Explain the architecture. Round 1 (janitor, constitution, jury, health score) lost the room on concepts.
- Show a view of what a named colleague knows. Person views are self-only.
- Pitch a trust score for documents. That's the crowded middle.
- Present a scenario number as a measurement, or a guess about SD Worx as a fact.
- Add a capture source after 14:00.

## Q&A prep

| They ask | We answer |
|---|---|
| How do you identify the gaps? (asked by someone at SD Worx today) | A gap is work the AI keeps doing that no human has reasoned about lately, or that only one person ever did. We count the human moments (decided, corrected, explained why) on similar past work, let them fade over 90 days, and flag what drops to zero or to one person. Then show it, don't explain it: the Aikido scan, 6 times Claude, 1 human, 19 days ago. |
| Where does the one hour a week come from? | It's an assumption: one hour of the 5.3 that people lose waiting on colleagues' knowledge. The pilot measures the real number: time from question to the right person. |
| How do people actually use it? Can our own agents call it? | In Slack or the AI assistant they already use. Under the hood it's a standard connector (MCP), so Claude, Copilot or SD Worx's own agents can ask "who knows" too. |
| How is this different from an expert finder, or from Vulcan Kinetics? | They find who wrote or who answered. In an AI company, that's often who prompted. We separate what the human brought from what the AI supplied. |
| Is this surveillance? | Raw sessions never leave the laptop, only the reasoning moments. You install it yourself and see your own view first. Leaders see what's going dark, not what a named colleague knows. |
| Isn't the tool itself high-risk under the AI Act? | It tells you who to ask. It never feeds performance reviews, promotion or task allocation. Using it for that would put it on the high-risk list for employment (Annex III, point 4(b)), which is why there's no score per person and leaders see topics, not people. |
| What if the AI was right and I accepted it? | Fine. Accepting isn't punished, it just isn't evidence you could have done it without the AI. |
| SD Worx consultants don't use Claude. | Any agent log has the same shape: the agent proposes, a human accepts or corrects. Agentic payroll already records where humans validate. |
| Why would anyone bother writing the why? | They already do. It's in the message when they overrule the AI. We only keep it. |
| How accurate is the classification? | Fill in with a real number from Tristan's tests or a hand check on our rows. Don't guess on stage. |
| What about knowledge that never touches AI? | Meetings and colleagues answering colleagues are the next source. Today the gap is biggest exactly where AI is used most. |
