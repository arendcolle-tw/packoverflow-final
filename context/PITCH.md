# Pitch: the story

What we pitch, what we say, what we show and what we leave out. First version by Wout, 20 Oct around 13:30. When #hackathon-finale decides otherwise, the channel wins and this file gets updated.

## In one breath

PackOverflow shows who still understands something, now that AI does the work. It reads the AI sessions people already have, counts only the moments a human overrules, decides or explains why, and answers "who knows" inside the Claude you already use.

- **Hook:** "The author is no longer the expert."
- **Tagline:** "Your AI can answer. We show who still understands."
- **5-second visual:** two names side by side. Left, in blue: who you'd ask (the most active person, the doc owner). Right, in amber: who actually knows (corrected Claude twice, with their reason in their own words). Same color logic as `DESIGN.md`: blue is the machine, amber is the human who knows.

## The case, stripped down

SD Worx's finals question: how does knowledge stay trusted when nobody maintains it? Learn from signals everyday work already creates. Often the answer is a person. Ask a human only where their judgement adds value. Their line: "zero unnecessary effort".

What nobody in the field says out loud: the everyday work that creates those signals now runs through AI. SD Worx already has 500 consultants coordinating payroll agents.

## The insight

Every way a company finds "who knows" runs on footprint: who wrote the doc, who owns it, who answered the thread. Expert finders and skill inference all work like this.

AI breaks footprint. When Claude writes the doc, the email and the Slack answer, the author can be the person who understands it least. Output stopped being evidence of knowledge.

What AI can't fake is the moment a human overrules it, decides, or explains why. That's where knowledge shows, and it's the only thing we count.

Against the field: most teams score documents on freshness, owner and conflicts. We say documents will be fine, because AI writes flawless ones. What quietly disappears is understanding. That's the brief's "think beyond documents", taken literally.

## Tomorrow: who uses it and how

Install: one URL in the Claude they already use. Recording runs in the background. Nothing to maintain.

- **A new developer at SD Worx** (the finals brief's own first example, and exactly what our real data is): "Who actually knows how we set this up here?" They get a person, that person's reason, and how recent it is.
- **A payroll consultant inheriting a client portfolio** (round-1 story 2): who really reasoned about this client, not who is listed as owner.
- **A team lead** before a holiday, a handover or an audit: "What's going dark?" Topics only one person understands, or nobody has reasoned about lately.
- **Each person, privately:** "What do I actually know, and what did I just prompt?"

Invisible when things are fine. It speaks up only when something goes dark.

## Where it goes: 10 to 20 years

- AI does most of the work and facts are free. The scarce thing becomes the person who can step in when the agent is wrong, a law flips overnight (Pay Transparency did), or a system breaks.
- Understanding erodes silently in three ways:
  - **Dormant systems** nobody has reasoned about in years, until an incident.
  - **The AI slop loop:** AI-written text feeds the next AI answer, and the human signal thins out.
  - **The broken apprenticeship:** who is SD Worx's senior PC 200 expert in 2040 if no junior reasons through a case in 2026?
- **Pilots solved this.** Under EASA rules (FCL.060) a pilot needs three take-offs and landings in the last 90 days before carrying passengers, because autopilot erodes skill. People who oversee AI have no such logbook. PackOverflow is that logbook, and our fade already runs on 90 days.
- **Every correction does two jobs:** it teaches the agent (SD Worx's own Legal Watch principle, experts' corrections feed back into the system) and it proves who knows.
- **Agents become users.** When a payroll agent isn't sure, it asks "who knows" and escalates to the right human. The connector already is that interface, and it works for any agent, not only Claude.

## Why SD Worx

- Their AI promise is "humans validate where it matters". That only holds while those humans still understand. We make it measurable: are the validators still reasoning, or clicking accept?
- AI Act, Article 14(4)(a): the people assigned to oversee a high-risk system must be able to "properly understand" its capacities and limitations. HR counts as high-risk.
- It's a layer on top of agentic payroll, Legal Watch and mysdworx, not a competitor.

## Words

**Say:** who knows, going dark, in their own words, just accepted, corrected, the why.

**Don't say on stage:** knowledge graph, proficiency, decay, takeover, signals, rows, task versus topic, TF-IDF, MCP (say "a connector in your Claude"), dark, fading and single point as three separate states (say "going dark").

## What we show and what we don't

**Show:** one path. The moment of doubt, the two names, live "who knows" and "what's going dark" on our own data, then the vision. The two-names beat needs one real topic where the busiest person is not the one who knows. That topic is the demo.

**Don't show:** the inside of the pipeline (record, graph and connector boxes), scores, the fade maths, or extra sources (screen capture, Copilot, M365, meetings, a Slack bot). Each one is a roadmap line or a Q&A answer.

## Do's and don'ts

**Do**
- Say what it is and show it working in the first 45 seconds. The organisers' feedback on round 1: it wasn't clear what we actually had.
- Demo on real data, our own sessions, and say so.
- Put the live URL, screenshots and a recording of the demo path in the PDF. We pitch on the organisers' device.
- Use SD Worx words: payroll consultant, client portfolio, PC 200, agentic payroll, Legal Watch, mysdworx.
- Mark every illustration as one.

**Don't**
- Explain the architecture. Round 1 (janitor, constitution, jury, health score) lost the room on concepts.
- Show a view of what a named colleague knows. Person views are self-only.
- Pitch a trust score for documents. That's the crowded middle.
- Add a capture source after 14:00.
- Present a guess about SD Worx as a fact.

## Q&A prep

| They ask | We answer |
|---|---|
| How is this different from an expert finder, or from Vulcan Kinetics? | They find who wrote or who answered. In an AI company, that's often who prompted. We separate what the human brought from what the AI supplied. |
| Is this surveillance? | Raw sessions never leave the laptop, only the reasoning rows. You install it yourself and see your own view first. Leads see what's going dark, not what a named colleague knows. |
| What if Claude was right and I accepted it? | Fine. Accepting isn't punished, it just isn't evidence you could have done it without Claude. |
| SD Worx consultants don't use Claude. | Any agent log has the same shape: the agent proposes, a human accepts or corrects. Agentic payroll already records where humans validate. |
| Why would anyone bother writing the why? | They already do. It's in the prompt when they overrule Claude. We only keep it. |
| How accurate is the classification? | Fill in with a real number from Tristan's tests or a hand check on our rows. Don't guess on stage. |
| What about knowledge that never touches AI? | Meetings and colleagues answering colleagues are the next source. Today the gap is biggest exactly where AI is used most. |
