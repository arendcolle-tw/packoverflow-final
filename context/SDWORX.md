- 100,000+ customer organisations; pays 6M+ employees every month; 10,000+ staff (round-1 brief).

## What SD Worx believes (their own words, paraphrased)
- **Past the experiment phase.** AI is now a structural lever for quality, compliance and customer experience, not a pilot (Gille Sebrechts, Chief Products & Technology Officer; Michael Custers, Chief Strategy Officer).
- **Hybrid, not replacement.** Humans plus AI beat either alone. Consultants move from repetitive data work to advice and proactive customer contact.
- **"Co-intelligence with full transparency."** Professionals must be able to review AI output and check its sources. Clear line between what's automated and what a human signs off.
- **AI handles scale and speed, humans give judgement** (Legal Watch). Experts' corrections feed back into the system.
- **Innovation is a means, not a goal.** It has to show measurable customer outcomes (Custers).
- **Pay transparency should lead to real pay equity**, not just compliance.
- **Governance and training, not just tech.** An AI ethics policy is a start; you need clear rules, accountability and people who can use AI confidently (Bruce Fecheyr Lippens, Chief People Officer).

## Their market right now
- **EU Pay Transparency Directive** applies since 7 June 2026: no more asking candidates their pay history, pay-gap reporting. Lots of internal docs became wrong overnight.
- **EU AI Act** high-risk rules (HR use cases count) apply from 2 Aug 2026; a delay to Dec 2027 has been proposed but not approved.
- SD Worx HR & Payroll Pulse 2026 (5,936 HR leaders, 16,500 employees, 16 countries):
  - 51% of HR teams now invest in AI (38% a year ago); ~4 in 10 already see clear results.
  - Payroll is a top AI priority, but most want **partial automation with human oversight** (40% for payroll).
  - Main blockers: processes not ready for AI (36%), no in-house expertise (32%), no clear business case (30%).
  - Employee trust depends on payroll accuracy and clear pay.

## Round-1 case (expect something related)
**"Unlock the Knowledge Within — Find it. Understand it. Trust it."**
How might we turn fragmented organisational knowledge into a trusted shared resource?

- Knowledge lives in policies, manuals, checklists, emails, Teams, workflows, business apps and people's heads.
- Finding is easy. The hard part: is this answer **reliable, current, and relevant to this customer, country or situation?**
- Their two stories:
  1. Urgent customer question. AI finds three docs: one recently updated, one without an owner, one maybe for another country. A colleague contradicts it in Teams.
  2. A payroll consultant inherits a client portfolio. The handover was one document and a one-hour chat, years ago.
- They asked for: a **focused** proof of concept (one role, one workflow, one source or one trust signal); make the **moment of doubt** tangible; trust that is **visible and explainable**, no black box; start from the friction, not the tech.
- Inspiration areas: **Trust** (is it reliable?), **Capture** (knowledge out of inboxes and heads), **Detect** (conflicting, duplicated, missing, outdated), **Connect** (find the right expert when documents aren't enough).
- SD Worx may use solutions in their own work.

## What this means for us
- Build **on top of** what they already run (agentic payroll, Legal Watch, mysdworx), not a competitor to it. Example phrasing: "a trust layer your 500 consultants' agents can call".
- Show the human in the loop clearly, with sources a consultant can check. That's their stated principle.
- Use their pressures as the story: Pay Transparency Directive, indexation, portfolio handovers, AI Act.
- Our edge: **who knows what**. The Connect area is where TechWolf lives (skills and expertise inferred from real work). In round 1 most teams used "the document owner"; the finalists who did people well stood out. Note Innovapay is SAP-integrated, a natural bridge if relevant.
