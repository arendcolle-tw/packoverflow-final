# Long-term vision: who still understands this?

Knowledge is not facts. A fact is traceable to a document or a message. Knowledge is a person who can reason about it and knows why it is the way it is. Once Claude answers most questions, facts stay easy to find, and knowledge quietly disappears.

## The 30-second beat

> This morning we ran the clock on our own Claude sessions. Our Aikido security setup: Claude handled it six times on its own. The last time a human reasoned about it was 19 days ago, and it was one person.
>
> That's four people after one day. Now picture SD Worx after ten years.
>
> Claude answered 400 questions about the mainframe this year. A human reasoned about it zero times. The one person who did retires in March.

The first part is real (`/api/topics/aikido-security-scan` on our data). The mainframe line is an illustration, say it as "picture", not as an SD Worx fact.

## What goes wrong in 10 to 20 years, and how the clock measures it

| Risk | What the clock shows |
|---|---|
| **Dormant knowledge.** A system runs for years, everyone asks Claude, then a security incident hits and nobody understands it. | Days since a human last reasoned about it. |
| **AI slop.** Knowledge bases fill with AI-written text that feeds the next AI answers. | Only human reasoning resets the clock. AI-written text never counts. |
| **Single points of knowledge.** One person holds it, goes on holiday or retires. | How many people ever reasoned about it. One is a red flag. |
| **The missing why.** Docs record what and how, rarely why. | Every reset keeps the person's own words about why. |

## The jury's five questions

1. **What happens automatically?** The clock is computed from Claude sessions (and later meetings) people already have. Nobody documents anything.
2. **When is human verification genuinely valuable?** When a human overrules Claude. That is where a decision is made, so that is the only moment we ask for one line of why.
3. **How do you keep maintenance from becoming a burden?** There is none. The clock moves on its own, and silence is the signal.
4. **How does it fit the workflow?** It reads the tools people already use. A lead looks at one screen when something goes dark.
5. **How does it stay understandable?** Every status is explained by the signals behind it, in people's own words. No score, no black box.

## Why SD Worx should care

- **Their own consultants.** Around 500 Belgian consultants already work with agentic payroll, where agents handle knowledge retrieval. The clock shows which payroll topics still have a human who understands them, before a joint-committee rule change or an audit asks.
- **Their customers.** SD Worx serves 100,000+ organisations with HR. A critical-skills and succession view built from real work signals, not surveys, fits on top of mysdworx.
- **The AI Act.** HR is a high-risk area and needs human oversight. You can only oversee what someone still understands.
